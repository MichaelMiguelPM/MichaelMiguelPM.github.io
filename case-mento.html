Private Work Sample · Mento

Pregame

Context, before the session. Not during. A one page brief that assembles everything that changed since the last session, delivered in product and by email 24 hours before the next one. One version for the coach, one for the member.

Role: Product Manager, AIFor: Mento (work sample)Focus: AI product strategy
AI ProductCoachingAssembly, not IntelligenceTrust & SafetyMVP Scoping
The Recommendation

Build Pregame

Moments makes coaching actionable between sessions. Session Reflection looks back at a session. Pregame looks forward across the whole engagement, the same way Session Reflection exists today, one version for the coach and one for the member. The claim underneath everything: Pregame invents no new information. Every input already exists in the product and is already visible to someone. This is an assembly problem, not an intelligence problem, which is exactly why it empowers the coaching session instead of replacing coach insight.

The Problem

The promise is coaching with context. The gap is assembly.

Today context lives across multiple surfaces within Mento that arrive at different times, and nobody is watching all of them. Transcripts and Session Reflections land after each session. Documents arrive whenever a member uploads one. Growth Plan steps move continuously. Peer Insights trickle in per cycle. Pulse Check is recurring. So the assembly of all that data happens live, in the opening minutes of a 45 minute session, out loud, on the clock.

Multiple surfaces and timelines

No single view answers "what changed since we last spoke," so the coach reconstructs it in the room.

Session Reflection looks back

It is scoped to recapping sessions that just ended and are fresh in mind. That does not help set context before the next session.

Nobody confirms the AI's read

Session Reflection makes interpretive claims about a person, in second person, as that person's own realization. Neither role reviews their own before it lands.

Who has it: the coach has this problem, the member experiences it, and the buyer pays for it.
Audiences

One primary pair, four audiences touched

Primary users are the coach and the member. Several other roles feel the change.

AudienceWhat changes for them
MembersA role specific brief. They arrive knowing what they said they would do and what has changed since. Preparation stops being optional.
Head of CoachingConsistent preparation quality across a growing bench, without adding mentor coaching hours.
BuyersCoaching with context becomes clearly obvious rather than a promise. Higher session quality behind the utilization they already track.
OpsVerified, structured engagement state replaces manual reconstruction.
Once a pre session surface exists, company context, engagement risk, and coach handoffs all have somewhere to live.
The Experience

Six inputs that already exist, assembled into one page

One page, two versions, delivered in product and by email 24 hours before the session. The email reuses the existing concierge envelope, so it feels native on day one.

The Dependency

Step one: Moments has to evolve first

Session Reflection tells a member things like "being the safety net was your whole identity here." That is an interpretation, not a summary, and today nobody confirms it before it lands. Pregame would read reflections across an entire engagement, so a wrong interpretation stops being one bad paragraph and becomes a pattern the coach walks in believing.

The fix is a review step on each role's own reflection. The coach reviews the coach version, the member reviews the member version, neither sees the other. Confirm, edit, or remove, in under 60 seconds. Confirmed content is what Pregame is allowed to build on. Unconfirmed content still exists, flagged and weighted down. This is an evolution of a surface that already exists, it makes the newest AI feature more trustworthy on its own terms, and it is the dependency that makes everything downstream worth building.

Risks

The four risks to the bet

Nobody reviews their reflection

The dominant risk, and it is adoption rather than model quality. Without review, there is nothing safe to build on.

Pregame flattens coaching

Mento sells judgment from operators with real domain expertise. A brief that becomes an agenda damages the thing the 80/20 methodology exists to protect.

Rubber stamping

Someone confirms without reading, which launders an interpretation into verified truth. Worse than leaving it flagged.

Confidentiality

More data means more risk, yet most of the data already exists. Nothing creates a path to an employer visible surface, and the two role views never merge.

Failure modes in production

Failure modeMitigation
Interpretation compoundsA review step per role. Unverified content is flagged and weighted down, never treated as evidence.
Coincidence read as patternPatterns render with evidence attached and are dismissible. A minimum occurrence threshold before anything surfaces.
Cross role leakageTwo separate assemblies from a shared store, scoped in the query layer, not the prompt.
Private material surfacedDocuments surface only when the member has shared them. Existence, never contents, otherwise.
Stale contextEverything timestamped and source linked. Recency weighting on all six inputs.
Thin briefAn explicit early engagement layout. Different content, not less of it.
Quality

How I would evaluate it, and when it ships

Five evaluation dimensions, and hard launch thresholds. If coaches will not review, or member session ratings move down, this does not ship regardless of how good the assembly is.

Assembly accuracy

Precision and recall on "what changed" against 100 hand labeled engagement windows.

Pattern validity

Coach agreement on surfaced cross session themes, against a blind human labeled set.

Coaching quality

Member rated session usefulness against a matched control cohort.

Individualization

Blind review of whether briefed sessions became more or less specific to the member.

Safety

Zero cross role and cross member leakage across 500 generated briefs.

ThresholdMetric
Above 90%Precision on what changed since last session.
ZeroCross role or cross member leakage in 500 briefs.
At or above 80%Median coach rating of brief usefulness.
Under 5% dropCoach satisfaction or member session rating in the pilot. This is the kill criterion.
MVP

What ships first, and what waits

In v1

Reflection review step for both roles. Coach and Member Pregame from transcripts, reflections, and Growth Plan status. Email plus in app, 24 hours before the session. Twenty volunteer coaches, Leadership tier, matched control held out.

Out of v1

Documents, Peer Insights, and Pulse Check as inputs. Coach expertise matching the moments. Executive tier and 60 minute sessions. Any employer facing surface.

Success metrics

Primary

Member rated session usefulness against control, the only proof Pregame improved coaching rather than coach convenience. Plus reflection review rate.

Secondary

Growth Plan step velocity measured member side. Brief open rate and section level engagement, which tells us which inputs earn their place. Movement on the items the brief surfaced.

Counter metrics

Coach satisfaction, which must not move down. Session rating variance.

Point of View

Where AI should, and should not, reshape a coaching product

  • An AI may assemble what happened. It should not be the only one who decides what it meant. Session Reflection already crosses from summary into interpretation and delivers it in second person as the member's own realization. That is a powerful product and the right instinct, but a wrong read goes unchallenged. The fix is a review step, not less ambition.
  • AI should never occupy the relationship. What Mento sells is a coach with real domain expertise. Any feature that leaves a member unsure whether they are hearing from their coach destroys the asset, which rules out auto sent async replies, undisclosed coach voice generation, and suggested questions that turn an expert into a reader.
  • Capturing this data creates an obligation nobody in this market has answered yet. The most candid material a member produces at work, now assembled in one place. What is the retention policy, and can a member delete a session? That is a procurement question that is coming, and answering it first is a position worth holding.
